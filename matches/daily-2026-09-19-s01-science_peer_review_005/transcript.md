## pro_opening

Speaker: pro
Phase: opening
Time used: 4.515s
Token estimate: 199

Our burden is comparative: show that open post-publication review would improve scientific knowledge more than anonymous pre-publication review, not that anonymity has no value.

Open review makes criticism continuous, public, attributable, and attached to the evidence as it accumulates. That improves accessibility: readers can see not only a paper’s initial gatekeeping, but replication results, methodological objections, corrections, and unresolved uncertainty. It also shifts incentives from merely securing publication toward producing claims that withstand scrutiny. Individual researchers may rationally avoid public criticism when reputational costs are possible; systemically, however, visible, durable review creates accountability and a richer record for verification.

Anonymous pre-review can protect junior or dissenting reviewers and filter obvious flaws before dissemination—real advantages. But it is episodic, opaque, and often treats publication as a misleading endpoint. Open review need not replace all pre-review: retaining basic screening while making substantive evaluation public best supports discovery, correction, and cumulative knowledge.

## con_opening

Speaker: con
Phase: opening
Time used: 2.763s
Token estimate: 189

The comparison remains underspecified: does “open post-publication review” mean replacing anonymous review, or merely adding a public layer? If addition, the claim that it improves science *more* requires showing its benefits exceed added workload and participation bias.

Public attribution may incentivize careful criticism, but it can also make individual researchers rationally avoid challenging senior scientists, controversial findings, or fashionable claims. That is not just a reputational inconvenience: it selectively suppresses precisely the dissent needed for correction. Anonymous pre-review can surface fatal methodological flaws before weak evidence is amplified, while post-publication correction often arrives after citation, media, and funding momentum.

Open records improve accessibility only if experts actually contribute and readers can distinguish replication from speculation. Thus openness helps verification, but it does not establish reliable verification. The stronger policy is complementary: anonymous pre-screening for baseline validity, followed by transparent, structured post-publication review.

## pro_rebuttal

Speaker: pro
Phase: rebuttal
Time used: 3.164s
Token estimate: 188

The objection rightly identifies participation bias, but it does not defeat the comparison. Discovery incentives favor anonymous pre-review: authors need a gate that protects novel, uncertain work from premature dismissal. Verification incentives differ: critics and readers need an accessible, cumulative record showing which claims survived replication and which remain contested. Open post-publication review supplies that record, while anonymity can conceal disagreement and make publication look like resolution.

Nor must openness mean unmoderated commentary or abolishing screening. A baseline anonymous check can catch obvious fatal flaws; the question is where substantive epistemic judgment should live. Public, attributable review may reduce some dissent, but anonymous review also suppresses accountability and leaves later uncertainty difficult to locate. Structured public reviews, replication reports, and explicit confidence labels can mitigate noise. System-level knowledge improves when correction is durable and inspectable, even if individual participation is imperfect.

The pro case quietly changes the comparison: it now preserves anonymous screening while relocating “substantive” judgment publicly. That is a complementary hybrid, not evidence that open post-publication review is superior to anonymous pre-review. More importantly, a visible record is not necessarily a reliable one. Public review can overrepresent senior, confident, or career-safe critics, while junior researchers rationally avoid attributable challenges; confidence labels cannot manufacture missing expertise or replications. Pre-publication anonymity is especially valuable before weak evidence gains citations, media attention, and funding momentum. Post-publication review may eventually correct it, but correction is uneven and uncertainty remains costly to discover. Open review may improve accessibility for verification, yet the relevant comparison is knowledge quality per total effort: if participation is selective and delayed, its system-level gains do not establish superiority over anonymous review.

## pro_closing

Speaker: pro
Phase: closing
Time used: 2.850s
Token estimate: 192

The closing burden is comparative system-level knowledge quality, not whether open review is flawless or costless. Con should concede that anonymous pre-review protects vulnerable dissenters and can catch obvious errors early; that makes it valuable for discovery. But discovery and verification require different incentives. Anonymous gatekeeping rewards persuading a small panel; open, durable review rewards claims that remain defensible as replications, corrections, and objections accumulate.

Public criticism may be selectively underprovided, yet anonymous review makes disagreement and uncertainty harder to inspect, while publication falsely appears final. Structured moderation, replication reports, and preserved dissent can reduce noise without pretending every comment is expert evidence. The relevant gain is not guaranteed participation in every case, but an accessible evidential history that lets later researchers update rather than inherit opaque verdicts. Thus a baseline screen may remain, but open post-publication review should carry the larger epistemic burden.

The pro side still has not established superiority; it has described a useful complement. An accessible evidential history matters only when qualified people contribute, yet attributable criticism predictably deters junior researchers and challenges to powerful or fashionable claims. Nor does a durable record undo early amplification: citations, funding, and public belief can accumulate before replication, while later corrections are unevenly noticed.
