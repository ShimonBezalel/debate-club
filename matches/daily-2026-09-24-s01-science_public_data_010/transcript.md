## pro_opening

Speaker: pro
Phase: opening
Time used: 7.585s
Token estimate: 167

Publicly funded research data should be open by default because the public both finances and depends on the resulting knowledge. Openness improves verification: independent researchers can test analyses, identify errors, assess uncertainty, and replicate findings rather than treating published conclusions as opaque authority. That strengthens the evidence base even when results are null or inconvenient.

This does not deny individual incentives. Researchers may reasonably fear being scooped, misinterpreted, or burdened with curation. But those are design problems—embargoes, privacy protections, documentation standards, and credit—not reasons for permanent secrecy. Individual rationality can favor withholding data, while system-level rationality favors access: science advances when evidence is reusable and contestable. “By default” also permits justified exceptions for privacy, security, or genuinely infeasible release, while making accessibility the starting presumption.

## con_opening

Speaker: con
Phase: opening
Time used: 3.734s
Token estimate: 168

The burden is not to show that openness is sometimes useful, but that it should be the default despite its costs. Discovery incentives and verification incentives differ: researchers need time, credit, and protection from premature or misleading reuse, while the system needs reliable, accessible evidence. A default release can weaken the former even as it strengthens the latter, especially when data are costly to curate, context-dependent, or vulnerable to misinterpretation. Embargoes and exceptions help, but they do not eliminate these burdens or guarantee adequate documentation. Nor does public funding settle ownership: funding supports a project, not necessarily unrestricted reuse of every dataset. Openness should therefore be encouraged and required where feasible, but a presumption of release risks optimizing visible reanalysis over careful discovery and responsible stewardship.

## pro_rebuttal

Speaker: pro
Phase: rebuttal
Time used: 2.637s
Token estimate: 169

The objection identifies real costs, but it does not establish that secrecy is the safer default. “Open by default” need not mean immediate, context-free release: staged access, embargoes, metadata, codebooks, and controlled access can protect discovery and participants while preserving verifiability. The relevant comparison is not individual convenience versus no cost; it is temporary curation burdens versus a system in which errors, selective reporting, and irreproducible claims remain inaccessible. Discovery incentives matter, but so do incentives to produce auditable evidence: credit, preregistration, and embargoes can reward both. Public funding does not imply unlimited reuse, but it does justify a presumption that publicly supported evidence should be accessible unless concrete privacy, security, consent, or feasibility reasons rebut it. “Where feasible” becomes meaningful only when openness is the baseline.

## con_rebuttal

Speaker: con
Phase: rebuttal
Time used: 4.087s
Token estimate: 189

The strongest case for openness is verification, but verification is not costless access: it requires interpretable, well-documented data and responsible reuse. A default can create pressure to release before those conditions exist, shifting resources from discovery and stewardship toward rapid reanalysis. That matters epistemically: inaccessible data can impede replication, but poorly contextualized data can generate false contradictions and undermine trust.

Staged or controlled access mitigates some risks, yet it does not solve who bears curation costs, how long embargoes should last, or when documentation is sufficient. Individual researchers may rationally protect scarce time and participant obligations; system-level knowledge quality depends on preserving those incentives, not merely maximizing datasets available for inspection. Public funding supports accountability, but does not make unrestricted reuse the only accountable policy. The better default is required sharing when justified and feasible, with access conditions determined by data-specific risks.
