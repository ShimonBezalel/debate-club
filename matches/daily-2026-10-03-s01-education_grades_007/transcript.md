## pro_opening

Speaker: pro
Phase: opening
Time used: 3.581s
Token estimate: 180

The burden is not to show that numbers are useless, but that narrative feedback should be the default for most schoolwork. A grade summarizes performance; comments can identify what a learner understands, where reasoning breaks down, and what to try next. That makes feedback more actionable and supports durable understanding, rather than merely optimizing for the next score.

This need not mean abolishing grades: retain them where comparison, certification, or quick tracking matters. Nor should every task require a long essay from the teacher. Brief, focused comments, peer review, and selective grading can limit workload; accessible formats and clear criteria help students with different needs. Some learners value numerical targets, and some subjects benefit from precise scoring, so exceptions are appropriate. But when the goal is learning, feedback should lead with guidance, not a number.

## con_opening

Speaker: con
Phase: opening
Time used: 2.500s
Token estimate: 179

The case assumes comments reliably produce better learning, but that is not established: vague or delayed narratives can be less useful than a clear score tied to criteria. A score may help students spot short-term gaps; comments may support deeper revision, but neither alone guarantees durable understanding. Replacing grades as the default also risks motivation and access: some learners need explicit benchmarks, and students may interpret narrative judgments inconsistently without substantial guidance. Workload is not solved by calling comments “brief”—individualized feedback takes time, especially across large classes, and peer review cannot replace teacher expertise in every subject. The right balance varies: essays invite targeted comments, while routine practice or objectively scored work may benefit from quick results. Improve feedback where it helps, but do not presume one format should replace numbers for most work.

That protects teacher time and preserves short-term tracking without mistaking performance on one task for durable understanding. Narrative-first feedback, with accommodations and exceptions, is the stronger default.
